A fence is the first thing a would-be intruder has to deal with, and the first thing a visitor reads about how seriously a property takes its own security. Done well, it does both jobs at once — deterring the wrong kind of attention without making a home or business look like a compound.

## Height and visibility do the real work

Most residential break-ins are opportunistic. A fence tall enough to require real effort to climb, combined with sightlines that don't give an intruder cover to work unseen, removes the easy option. For commercial sites — warehouses, equipment yards, storage lots — that same principle scales up: taller ornamental steel or chain-link with slats, positioned so approach routes are visible from the building or the street.

## Ornamental steel and aluminum

For a home or a business that wants security without the look of a prison yard, ornamental steel is the standard answer. Closely spaced pickets are hard to climb or squeeze through, and the finish reads as an intentional design choice rather than a defensive measure bolted on as an afterthought.

## Gates and access points

A fence is only as secure as its weakest gate. Keypad entry, auto-close hinges, and matching the gate's material and height to the rest of the run all matter more than most property owners expect — a beautiful fence with a sagging, unsecured gate advertises the gap.

## Commercial and industrial

Warehouses and storage yards typically pair height with deterrents: anti-climb mesh, angled toppers where local code allows, and clear zones along the perimeter that keep the fence line visible to cameras and passing staff.
